How it works, step by step

These are the same steps as the notebook, each with a live demo. Each one covers what we are looking for, how to read it, what it means, and what's next.

Step 1: One roll of the die

What we're looking for: How does a single die roll decide the next move?

—

How to read it: The die and the move it causes are shown below. A 6 triggers a second roll that adds that many steps.

What it means: Each roll is random, but the rules turn it into a move: 1–2 down, 3–5 up one, 6 climb by a second roll (1–6). Going down can never take you below step 0.

What's next: A single roll tells you almost nothing about the bet. The next step strings 100 rolls together.

Step 2: One random walk

What we're looking for: Where does one climb of 100 rolls end up?

How to read it: The line is your step number after each roll. The dashed line is the target of 60.

What it means: This is a random walk: each step depends on the previous one, which is what makes it a walk and not just a list of random numbers. One walk is one possible story, and it can end above or below the target.

What's next: One story is not an answer. Step 3 repeats the walk thousands of times.

Step 3: Thousands of walks

What we're looking for: How are the final steps spread out over many climbs?

How to read it: Each bar counts walks that ended in that range of steps. Bars right of the dashed target line are wins.

What it means: The spread is the key idea of hacker statistics: simulate the game many times and read the odds from the pile of results. The share of walks ending at or above 60 is the estimated chance.

What's next: So far nobody ever falls. Step 4 adds clumsiness.

Step 4: Add clumsiness

What we're looking for: How much does a 0.1% chance of falling to step 0 on each roll hurt the odds?

How to read it: Two bars: the win chance with no clumsiness, and with the 0.1% fall chance used in the notebook.

What it means: A fall is rare per roll, but over 100 rolls it happens to roughly one walk in ten, and it wipes out the progress made so far. That lowers the odds noticeably.

What's next: Step 5 turns the result into one number to bet on.

Step 5: The answer

What we're looking for: What are the chances of winning the bet?

How to read it: Chance of winning = walks that reached the target ÷ total walks.

What it means: With the notebook's settings the answer comes out around 78% (exactly 77.7%). Because it is simulated, each run differs by a little; more walks makes it steadier.

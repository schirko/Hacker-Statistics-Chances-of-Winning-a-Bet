Game · Optimal stopping

Roll or stop?

Climb the same stairs as the simulator, but now you decide when to quit. After every roll you can roll again or stop. The page knows the exact best move at every position and tells you how close your choices came.

The rules

  • Prize: stop on step 60 or higher and you collect 100 points.
  • Quit: stop below the target and you walk away with nothing.
  • Cost: every roll costs 0.5 points. The game also ends by itself after 100 rolls.
  • The dice: 1–2 step down, 3–5 step up one, 6 roll again and climb that many. Each roll also has a 0.1% chance of a clumsy fall to step 0.

Your score is the prize (if you collect it) minus the fees you paid. The best possible plan averages 42.6 points per game, and it does not always win the prize.

What "expected loss" means: at each decision, the exact method knows how many points rolling and stopping are each worth on average. If you choose the worse one, the difference is what that choice cost you in expectation, whatever the dice then do. Luck decides one game; expected loss measures the decisions.

How the best move is found

What we're looking for: at every position (roll number, step) is it better to roll or to stop?

How it works: start at roll 100, where the game ends by itself and the payoff is known. Work backwards one roll at a time. At each position, compare the payoff of stopping now with the payoff of rolling once more, which is minus the fee plus the average best value of the positions you might reach. The bigger one is the best move. This is the same backwards recursion as the value map in the lab, with a choice at every step.

What it means: the best rule has three parts. Collect the prize as soon as you reach the target. Quit once you are too far behind for the rolls that are left (the red area on the map). Roll otherwise. Notebook 05 solves this in Python and then teaches a program to find the rule by trial and error with Q-learning.
